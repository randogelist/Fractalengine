The minimum32 runs used 201/209 completion batches and 193/205 sleeps;
3020.346/3347.690ms were spent in completion waits. This confirms that host
wake delay caused the adaptive policy to collapse into tiny batches.
Even 10x6 previews spent roughly 15ms waiting for one GPU completion.

CPU repairs: 11338/14400 (78.74%) for Cayley currents and 7380/14400 (51.25%)
for Tricorn weave. Mixed precision had exactly 28800/57600 repairs.
Its repair stage scaled from 15.451ms at one worker to 1.576ms at 16 workers,
and 1.464ms at 32. Waiting still dominated overall GPU frame time.

Split trials had CPU work lasting 0.8-3.7ms but total frames lasting
45.8-77.9ms, dominated by the GPU branch and single-worker repairs.
These results do not justify enabling that split in the GUI.

CPU hashes agree across workers. GPU hashes are deterministic but differ from
CPU hashes on these long chaotic views. Even one channel level can change a
hash. The short numerical fixtures do not establish full-view image agreement;
the next run reports explicit image-channel differences outside timing.

Process CPU time advances in coarse ticks. Zero CPU time for a 2ms frame
cannot establish zero CPU usage.
